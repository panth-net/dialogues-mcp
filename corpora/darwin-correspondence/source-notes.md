# Source Notes

This corpus draws on two kinds of source, at two levels of editing.

## Francis Darwin edited collections (public domain, bulk layer)

Public-domain Project Gutenberg editions edited by Darwin's son Francis Darwin (and, for *More Letters*, with A. C. Seward):

- *The Life and Letters of Charles Darwin*, Volumes 1-2 (Gutenberg 2087, 2088).
- *More Letters of Charles Darwin*, Volumes 1-2 (Gutenberg 2739, 2740).
- *The Autobiography of Charles Darwin* (Gutenberg 2010).

These are the main searchable body. They are rights-clear public domain, and users download them locally themselves. Note the editorial bias: these are Victorian family editions. Francis Darwin and his collaborators softened tone, abridged, and in places omitted material, especially on religion, doubt, and personal conflict. The *More Letters* volumes are arranged by subject, not strict chronology, so date fields on these items are coverage spans rather than exact letter dates. Treat the edited text as reliable for the substance of Darwin's working science but not as a complete or unshaped record of the man.

## Darwin Correspondence Project letter items (modern-edition layer)

Twelve individually selected letters keyed to the Darwin Correspondence Project (darwinproject.ac.uk), preserved with letter number, sender, recipient, date, and source URL. These are modern scholarly transcriptions of single hinge-point letters. Where they overlap the edited volumes, prefer the DCP text.

## Rights, provenance, and limitations

No source text is bundled. Users download permitted public-source texts locally after reviewing source terms; the Gutenberg volumes are public domain and the DCP items are user-downloaded from the original source. Preserve letter number, sender, recipient, date, source URL, and correspondent metadata where present. Some marginalia or edited texts may have access or rights limits; mark those as catalog-only or user-supplied when needed. Coverage is partial: much of Darwin's correspondence, and the marginalia and reading records, are not included, and the Victorian editing means gaps and softening rather than neutral omission.
