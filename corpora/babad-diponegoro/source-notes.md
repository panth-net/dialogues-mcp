# Source Notes

The searchable core downloads from the Internet Archive item `babad-diponegoro` (uploaded 2023): a Latin-script romanized transliteration of the Babad Dipanagara manuscript LOr 6547, served as one plain-text file per canto under `LOr. 6547 Hazeu/Transliteration/`. Verified July 2026 by plain GET: canto 1 opens with the famous Mijil stanza written in Manado exile (Sun amědhar surasaning ati...), in clean stanza-per-block formatting with ě diacritics.

What downloads and what does not:

- 41 of 43 cantos download as UTF-8 text. Cantos 7 and 12 are 54-byte error stubs in the source ("Error: Generation stopped") and are excluded; that gap is real and answers should acknowledge it.
- The same archive.org item also carries Indonesian and English translation files. They are uncredited modern translation layers with unclear rights and are NOT downloaded.
- The British Library EAP1268 scans (the 1946 hand-written transliteration) sit behind a human-verification gate and remain catalog pointers; the Wikimedia Commons manuscript PDF is image-only with no text layer.
- Sastra.org was checked July 2026 and holds no Babad Dipanagara transcription; jv.wikisource has none either.

Rights posture, stated plainly:

- Diponegoro's 1831-1832 text is public domain.
- The downloaded transliteration layer is uncredited, carries no stated license on the archive.org item, and appears machine-generated from the pegon manuscript (the two error-stub cantos are the tell). Downloading therefore requires an explicit user confirmation, the text stays local for personal indexing, and it must not be redistributed or republished.
- The scholarly layers around the babad — modern critical transliterations, editions, and translations (notably Peter Carey's decades of Diponegoro scholarship) — are in copyright and are never downloaded; users who own them may import permitted text themselves.

Known limits: the transliteration's philological quality is unvetted — spellings, stanza divisions, and difficult readings should be checked against the EAP scans or a scholarly edition before bearing weight; the babad survives through manuscript copying, and LOr 6547 is one recension; romanization varies by era and school; and the chronicle is partisan testimony — Diponegoro's own account, written in exile after defeat, which is its value and its bias at once.

Imported and downloaded documents stay local, are never bundled, and are indexed only into a locally generated SQLite file.
