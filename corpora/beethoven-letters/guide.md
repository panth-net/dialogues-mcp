# Corpus Guide

## What this corpus is

This corpus is for Beethoven's letters as evidence of work under pressure: composition, publishing, copying, patrons, dedications, money, illness, deafness, family conflict, legal matters, reputation, and friendship.

The initial public-source edition is an English translation. Original German local imports are welcome; the AI can translate fetched passages, but answers must say what language and edition the evidence comes from.

## Where to start

- Ask what secret Beethoven begged an old friend to keep from every human being — a composer starting to lose his hearing, and terrified of what it would do to his name.
- Ask about the letter to his brothers from Heiligenstadt in 1802 — he admits how close deafness brought him to ending his life, and what held him back.
- Ask to read the letter that begins "My angel! my all! my second self!" — the "immortal beloved" letters, love at full pitch from a man who never married.
- Ask why his letters call his nephew Karl "my dear son" — he fought the boy's mother in court for years for the right to raise him.

## How to talk with this corpus

Do not turn Beethoven into only a heroic genius or a suffering stereotype. Look for practical problems: getting scores copied, negotiating publishers, depending on patrons, managing illness, fighting family battles, asking for money, revising work, and protecting reputation.

Distinguish letter evidence from later biography. A letter is a situated act: it has a recipient, purpose, and pressure.

## How to search

Start with concrete terms: publisher, score, copyist, sonata, quartet, symphony, opera, Archduke, Ries, Wegeler, brother, nephew, illness, deafness, money, florins, subscription, dedication, proof, arrangement.

For German local imports, search original terms when known and let the AI translate fetched passages in the answer.

## How to answer

Cite fetched passages and identify whether the evidence is from a translated public-domain edition or an original-language import. Quote translation sparingly and do not imply exact access to Beethoven's German unless the fetched passage is in German.

Explain practical context before psychological interpretation.

Some chunks include editorial notes around the letter text. Use those notes for edition context only. When answering about Beethoven's thought, make clear which claims come from Beethoven's own letter and which come from translator/editor framing.

## What to watch for

- Treating translated letters as transparent access to the original wording.
- Over-psychologizing illness or deafness from isolated lines.
- Ignoring recipients and letter purpose.
- Treating editorial arrangement as Beethoven's own structure.
- Forgetting money, publishers, copyists, and patrons as part of composition.
