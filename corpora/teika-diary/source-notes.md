# Source Notes

Meigetsuki survives in two states: sections in Teika's own hand — much of the autograph preserved in the Reizei family library in Kyoto, with detached sections in national museum collections — and later manuscript copies that carry the rest of the 56-year span. The autograph corpus is designated a National Treasure.

The two manifest records are e-Museum (National Institutes for Cultural Heritage, Japan) records for surviving autograph sections:

- A Tokyo National Museum section, catalogued as "Bright Moon Diary."
- A Kyoto National Museum section covering the first to third months of 1199 (Kenkyu 10), including Teika's detailed notice of the death of Shogun Minamoto no Yoritomo.

Both provide manuscript images with IIIF manifests and catalog descriptions. Neither provides a transcription, and this capsule downloads nothing from them. They are provenance pointers, not text.

Text must come from the user: a printed Japanese transcription — the Kokusho Kankokai edition (1911-1912) is the classic one, and later scholarly editions exist — must be obtained, and any digital text derived from it must be permitted before import. OCR of kanbun printed editions is error-prone; expect to correct character-recognition errors, and prefer keyed text where rights allow.

Language: the original is kanbun — Classical Chinese as written in Japan — not classical Japanese prose. Modern Japanese renderings (yomikudashi or full translation) and English excerpts are interpretations layered on the original; keep track of which layer an imported file contains. Rights for transcriptions and translations vary by edition and must be confirmed by the user; imported text stays local.
