# Source Notes

The manuscript diary — 51 volumes, 1779-1848 — is part of the Adams Papers at the Massachusetts Historical Society (MHS) in Boston.

What downloads here is the twelve-volume printed selection: Memoirs of John Quincy Adams, Comprising Portions of His Diary from 1795 to 1848, edited by his son Charles Francis Adams (Philadelphia: J. B. Lippincott, 1874-1877), fetched as OCR text from the Internet Archive. The edition is public domain. Two things follow from what it is:

- It is a selection, roughly 40 percent of the diary, made by Charles Francis Adams with a Victorian editor's cuts and discretion — family matters trimmed, repetitions dropped, judgments softened or omitted. Absence of a passage from the Memoirs is not absence from the diary.
- The text is uncorrected OCR of 1870s typography: running heads, page numbers, and misread characters intrude, and names, dates, and numbers can be mangled. Treat surprising readings as possible scan errors and verify.

The John Quincy Adams Digital Diary, published by the Primary Source Cooperative at MHS, is the complete verified scholarly transcription, searchable and readable online — the version to consult when the Memoirs omit a date, when wording matters, or when the OCR looks wrong. It offers no public bulk export, so its record here stays `catalog_only`: a provenance pointer, not passage evidence. The same is true of the MHS Adams Papers Digital Edition search portal.

Known limits to keep in mind:

- Adams kept parallel records: short "line-a-day" memoranda and long entries written up afterward, sometimes from those lines, so the same day can exist in two versions with different detail and hindsight.
- Coverage thins during illness and after his 1846 stroke; the last Memoirs entry is 4 January 1848.
- Volume I opens with the editor's memoir of the years before 1795; that framing text is Charles Francis Adams's voice, not the diary.
