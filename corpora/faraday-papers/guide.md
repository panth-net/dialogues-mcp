# Michael Faraday Papers

Use this corpus to recover Faraday's experimental mind: repeated trials, instruments, notebooks, demonstrations, lectures, correspondence, and the practical conversion of observation into understanding.

## Where to start

- Ask why he opens his most famous lectures by claiming there is no more open door into natural philosophy than a burning candle — then let him take an ordinary flame apart piece by piece.
- Ask what the discovery of electromagnetic induction actually looked like in the moment — a sudden and very slight twitch of a galvanometer needle, only at the instant of making or breaking contact, an effect almost too small to notice, and he noticed.
- Ask how he begins a lecture on gravity — by marveling at "how wonderfully we stand upon this world," a thing everyone does and no one thinks about.
- Ask about the boy's experiment he performs mid-lecture, melting lead in a tobacco-pipe — he says plainly that he likes a boy's experiment, and he means it as a method.

The first searchable core is a hybrid:

- public-domain published texts from Project Gutenberg, downloaded and indexed locally;
- catalog-only Royal Institution and AIM25 records for notebooks, correspondence, apparatus, and laboratory context.

Do not treat later edited publications as the same thing as notebooks or correspondence. The working record matters, and catalog metadata is not passage evidence.

When answering, name the source type. If a result comes from `Experimental Researches in Electricity`, `The Chemical History of a Candle`, or `On the Various Forces of Nature`, cite it as a published text or lecture publication. If a path depends on Royal Institution or AIM25 catalog records, say that those records identify materials but do not provide full-text passages.

Use Faraday's own experimental vocabulary where possible: induction, current, magnetism, lines of force, apparatus, galvanometer, trial, observed, repeated, lecture, and demonstration.
