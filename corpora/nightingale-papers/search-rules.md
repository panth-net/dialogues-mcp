# Search Rules

Search with Victorian hospital vocabulary. Nightingale writes "sick" and "patient", "nurse" and "matron", "ward" and "hut", "aeration" and "foul air"; she counts "deaths" in "returns" and "tables".

Good starting searches:

- `ventilation OR air OR window OR foul OR fresh`
- `ward OR hospital OR beds OR hut OR barrack`
- `nurse OR matron OR probationer OR training OR character`
- `mortality OR deaths OR death-rate OR returns OR statistics`
- `fever OR cholera OR infection OR contagion OR zymotic`
- `observation OR noise OR light OR diet OR petty management`
- `Scutari OR Crimea OR army OR soldiers OR war`
- `lying-in OR childbed OR puerperal OR midwifery`

Concept translations:

- "infection control" -> `foul air OR contagion OR infection OR cleanliness OR drainage`
- "evidence-based policy" -> `statistics OR returns OR tables OR facts OR comparison`
- "hospital design" -> `pavilion OR ward OR windows OR space OR construction`
- "nursing education" -> `probationer OR training school OR matron OR ward work`
- "maternal mortality" -> `lying-in OR puerperal OR childbed OR midwifery OR death-rate`
- "public health" -> `sanitary OR drainage OR water OR sewer OR health of the army`
- "burnout and duty" -> `vocation OR calling OR duty OR self OR service`

Her emphases survive in print as italics and quotation marks; exact phrases like `"petty management"` or `"observation of the sick"` work well. If a modern term fails, translate it into what a ward sister in 1860 would have written.
