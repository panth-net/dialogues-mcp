# Lovelace, Babbage, and Early Computing

Use this corpus to recover computation as a collaborative documentary process: learning, correspondence, machine design, tables, diagrams, programs, notes, and contested credit.

## Where to start

- Ask how Ada Lovelace learned higher mathematics as an adult, by post — her letters to her tutor own their errors outright: "I now see exactly my mistake."
- Ask who caught the "grave mistake" in Babbage's own working of the famous Bernoulli calculation — by his own later account, it was Lovelace.
- Ask what Ada hoped to bequeath to the generations: a "Calculus of the Nervous System" — she wanted to do for the brain what Newton had done for physics.
- Ask why people still argue over how much of the celebrated 1843 paper was truly hers — this corpus holds the skeptics' case and her working letters side by side.

Avoid both caricatures: do not claim Ada alone invented programming, and do not claim Ada contributed nothing. Let the documents show learning, collaboration, dependency, and argument.

Some Babbage material may be metadata or image records without transcription. Mark that boundary clearly.
