# Source Notes

Provenance: Miranda's 63 bound volumes left La Guaira by sea in 1812 as the First Republic collapsed, reached England, and vanished into Lord Bathurst's papers until 1922, when they were found at Cirencester; Venezuela purchased them in 1926 and the Caracas printing followed from 1929 as the Archivo del General Miranda (24 volumes, 1929-1950). The underlying manuscripts are eighteenth- and early nineteenth-century and public domain; rights questions attach to editions, not to Miranda's own papers.

What downloads (all verified July 2026 by plain GET with content checks):

- archive.org OCR text (`_djvu.txt`) of seven published volumes: the 1928 Robertson edition of the 1783-1784 US travel diary (New York, pre-1930, public domain in the US; Spanish diary text); the 1794 London Original Correspondence between Generals Dumourier, Miranda, Pache and Beurnonville; Antepara's South American Emancipation (London, 1810); Biggs's History of Don Francisco de Miranda's Attempt (1811); Miranda en la revolución francesa (Caracas, 1889); Rojas's El general Miranda (Paris, 1884); and Fragments from an XVIII Century Diary (Caracas, 1931 — gray edition status, so it downloads under an explicit confirmation and stays local).
- The 1806 Coro proclamation from Spanish Wikisource via the REST HTML endpoint — the only substantive Miranda document transcribed there (the author page was checked; nothing else exists).

Why the Archivo itself does not download:

- The 24-volume Archivo has no machine-readable text anywhere found: archive.org searches (several title forms) return nothing. The only digitization is at HathiTrust (catalog record 008300057), which blocks bulk/programmatic download and 403s scripted catalog requests; Dialogues does not bypass that, so the record is a browser-only pointer.
- The modern Colombeia edition (Caracas, 1978- ) is recent scholarly work under copyright and is excluded entirely.
- The former Google Books pointers were removed: the scans are not programmatically verifiable and the HathiTrust record supersedes them.

Limits to keep in mind: the printed volumes are editions, with prefaces, ordering decisions, and occasional normalization between the reader and the manuscripts; the OCR garbles eighteenth-century typefaces (long-s as f), accents, and French passages; Biggs and Rojas are witnesses about Miranda, not Miranda; and the archive is Miranda's own curated case for himself — bias by design. Confirm the status of the 1931 Fragments edition in your jurisdiction before reusing beyond local indexing, and keep all downloads local.

Imported and downloaded documents stay local, are never bundled, and are indexed only into a locally generated SQLite file.
