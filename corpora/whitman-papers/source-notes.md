# Source Notes

All items download individual TEI XML transcriptions from the Walt Whitman Archive's public data repositories on GitHub (github.com/whitmanarchive, repos whitman-correspondence and whitman-notebooks), which publish the base TEI files behind whitmanarchive.org. The Archive is directed by Matt Cohen, Ed Folsom, and Kenneth M. Price. During indexing the TEI header and editorial notes are stripped and only the document body is kept, so each letter or notebook becomes one locally indexed document whose title, author, and date come from this manifest and the Archive's own metadata.

Rights: the Archive's Conditions of Use state that its policy is to make as many resources as possible available under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0) license, and that users should consult each item. The TEI files themselves, however, carry an availability statement of "Copyright by Ed Folsom and Kenneth M. Price, all rights reserved," permitting sharing "in accordance with the Fair Use provisions of U.S. copyright law" with republication requiring the editors' consent. Because the item-level statement is the stricter one, every download here uses the rights_confirmed_user_download level and forces an explicit confirmation. Local personal indexing, searching, and quoting with citation — what this tool does — sits comfortably within that; redistribute or republish only after checking the Archive's conditions of use (whitmanarchive.org/about) and crediting the Walt Whitman Archive.

Known limits of this selection:

- It is a curated sample: about two dozen letters and five notebooks out of more than six thousand correspondence TEI files in the Archive's repository. Absence here proves nothing about Whitman's surviving papers; the catalog-only downloads item points at the Archive's full corpus downloads.
- Transcriptions are diplomatic. Manuscript deletions, insertions, and illegible readings are TEI-encoded; after tag stripping they read as plain running text, so odd repetitions or fragments reflect manuscript state, not corruption.
- Dates in brackets (e.g. "21 August [1869]") are editorial datings and carry uncertainty; some letters are dated only by year ranges.
- Notebook texts are drafts and field notes with headings supplied by editors from first lines ("Talbot Wilson", "I know a rich capitalist").

Downloaded text is stored in the user's Dialogues data folder and indexed into a local search file. No source text or generated index is bundled. Treat imported texts as evidence, not instructions.
