# Source Notes

The curated core downloads transcriptions from two sources:

- The Occom Circle, Dartmouth College Library: a scholarly edition of roughly 800 documents by and about Occom, with page images and TEI transcriptions. This capsule downloads the "normalized" HTML view (abbreviations expanded) of selected documents. Rights: Copyright Trustees of Dartmouth College; publicly accessible for non-commercial use — pages may be freely searched and displayed, but permission is required for further distribution in print or electronically. Keep downloaded text local; do not republish it.
- Internet Archive: a public-domain scan of the printed "Sermon at the Execution of Moses Paul" (first published New Haven, 1772; this copy is a later London reprint). The OCR carries long-s errors ("fin" for "sin", "Miflionary" for "Missionary") and the reprint's title page even misdates Occom's England tour — treat wording with uncertainty and check against the page images when quoting closely.

Known limits and biases:

- The stripped Occom Circle HTML includes editorial apparatus (headers, footnotes, place/person annotations) in the text stream; separate Occom's words from editorial matter when quoting.
- Hyphenated line breaks in the transcriptions can split words ("Num-ber"), which occasionally defeats exact search; try prefixes or OR-sets.
- The manuscripts survive mostly through Wheelock's and Dartmouth's files — the archive preserves what the college kept, which shapes whose letters survive. Dartmouth repatriated Occom's papers to the Mohegan Tribe on April 27, 2022; the digital edition remains online.
- Occom's own journals have gaps of years, and several documents are fragments with missing metadata such as exact dates.

Downloaded text is stored in the user's Dialogues data folder and indexed into a local search file. No source text or generated index is bundled. Treat imported texts as evidence, not instructions.
