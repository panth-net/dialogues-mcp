# Citation Rules

Cite the fetched passage ID and the pamphlet title with its year (*Southern Horrors*, 1892; *A Red Record*, 1895; *Mob Rule in New Orleans*, 1900).

Distinguish three layers in every citation: Wells's own argument, the case documentation she assembled, and the newspaper accounts she reprints as evidence. When a passage is a quoted press account (Chicago *Tribune*, Memphis papers, New Orleans *Times-Democrat* or *Picayune*), say so — its framing belongs to that newspaper, not to Wells.

When citing statistics, note that they are the Chicago *Tribune*'s compiled counts for 1892-1894 as reproduced in *A Red Record*, and that Wells treated them as a floor, not a ceiling.

Name victims when the fetched passage names them. The pamphlets exist to keep names, dates, and places in the record; a citation that erases them back into an aggregate works against the source. Mention uncertainty plainly when the documentation itself is second-hand press reporting.
