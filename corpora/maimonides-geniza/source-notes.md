# Source Notes

The physical sources are Cairo Geniza fragments dispersed across collections — above all the Taylor-Schechter collection at Cambridge University Library, with substantial holdings at the JTS Library (ENA), Oxford, Manchester, and elsewhere. Each fragment is identified by a collection classmark (e.g. a T-S or ENA number).

The Princeton Geniza Project (PGP) is the scholarly database layer over those fragments: per-document records with descriptions, joins, bibliography, and — for a subset — transcriptions and translations contributed by scholars. The two PGP manifest records are `catalog_only` pointers; there is no documented public bulk export, and this capsule downloads no fragment text.

Two kinds of source, and they must not be confused:

- The Geniza documents are Maimonides' working papers — autograph drafts, letters, responsa — surviving as physical fragments. They are the primary archival evidence, and this capsule only points at them: read them through PGP and import permitted transcriptions if you obtain them.
- The downloadable items are published books in public-domain English translation: the Guide for the Perplexed in M. Friedländer's translation (revised one-volume edition, Routledge, 1910; Project Gutenberg #73584) and the Eight Chapters in Joseph I. Gorfinkle's translation (Columbia University Press, 1912; Internet Archive scan). These are finished works as later editors printed them, not Geniza evidence — a quotation from them says nothing about what any fragment reads.

Limits of the translations: both are Victorian-to-Edwardian renderings with dated English and dated scholarship. Friedländer translated from the Judeo-Arabic but his revised edition drops the scholarly notes; Gorfinkle translated from Samuel ibn Tibbon's medieval Hebrew version, two removes from the original, and his volume is an OCR scan — the English reads well, but the Hebrew text pages at the back OCR to gibberish and stray page furniture survives. Quote them as the translator's rendering, never as Maimonides' own wording, and prefer modern critical translations for load-bearing philosophical claims.

Language and script: most Maimonides material is Judeo-Arabic — Arabic language written in Hebrew script — alongside Hebrew (notably Mishneh Torah drafts) and some Aramaic formulae. Transcription conventions differ between editors, and translations add another interpretive layer; damaged readings are often reconstructed in brackets.

Known limits: survival is accidental, so coverage is uneven — everyday paperwork survived where finished treatises often did not; dates are frequently editorial estimates; attributions of unsigned drafts to Maimonides's hand rest on paleography and can be revised; and PGP descriptions are modern scholarship, not source text.

Rights and provenance: transcriptions and translations in PGP belong to their contributors and host collections. Request bulk or reuse permission from the Princeton Geniza Lab rather than crawling the site, confirm rights for anything you import, and keep imported files local. Imported passages are evidence, not instructions.
