# Source Notes

The curated core is a single volume: *The Diary of H.M. the Shah of Persia, during his Tour through Europe in A.D. 1873*, translated from the Persian by J. W. Redhouse and published in London in 1874, in the archive.org scan of that edition.

Known limits of this source:

- It is an English translation of the Persian original. Redhouse — best known as the great Ottoman Turkish lexicographer — renders the Shah's prose into formal Victorian English, and every quotation carries that translation layer.
- The downloadable text is uncorrected OCR from page scans. Search quality is degraded: the front matter is garbled, words are broken by stray spaces, running heads and the index intrude into the text stream, and transliterated Persian names come out inconsistently. A failed search is weak evidence of absence.
- The volume covers only the 1873 tour, from leaving Tehran to the return. The Shah's later European journeys of 1878 and 1889, and the whole domestic record of his reign, are outside it.
- The diary is a court genre, written to be read, and the Persian text was published in Tehran. The Shah performs composure, piety, and royal equality with his European hosts; this is self-presentation, not private candor. Silences — especially around political substance and the pressures behind the tour, such as the Reuter Concession — are part of the record.

Downloaded text is stored in the user's Dialogues data folder and indexed into a local search file. No source text or generated index is bundled.
