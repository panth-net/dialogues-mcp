# Search Rules

Use place names, ruler titles, and the diary's concrete object-words in Victorian spellings. The itinerary is the corpus's backbone: a city name is usually the fastest way into a leg of the tour.

Good starting searches:

- `Enzeli OR Caspian OR Astrakhan OR Volga OR steamer`
- `Moscow OR Petersburg OR "Winter Palace" OR Kremlin OR Tsar OR Emperor`
- `London OR Windsor OR Queen OR Woolwich OR Guildhall`
- `review OR fleet OR ironclad OR cannon OR guns OR ship`
- `railway OR train OR carriage OR tunnel OR telegraph`
- `ballet OR theatre OR opera OR danced OR performance`
- `Paris OR Versailles OR Marshal OR France`
- `Vienna OR exhibition OR Austria OR Berlin OR Prussia`

Useful path strategies:

Concept translations:

- "industrialization" -> `factory OR machine OR manufactory OR works OR steam OR iron`
- "military power" -> `review OR troops OR cannon OR fleet OR ironclad OR arsenal`
- "royal ceremony" -> `reception OR gala OR ball OR dinner OR salute OR procession`
- "culture shock" -> `strange OR wonderful OR astonishing OR curious OR never seen`
- "diplomacy" -> `ambassador OR minister OR audience OR visit OR treaty`
- "public entertainment" -> `ballet OR theatre OR opera OR gardens OR fireworks OR crowd`
- "infrastructure" -> `railway OR bridge OR tunnel OR telegraph OR road OR port`

Spelling notes: the 1874 translation and its OCR give `Teheran` (not Tehran), `Tsaritsin`, `St. Petersburg`, and inconsistent transliterations of Persian names. The OCR also breaks words with stray spaces, so if a phrase fails, search a single distinctive word from it, or an OR set of variants.
