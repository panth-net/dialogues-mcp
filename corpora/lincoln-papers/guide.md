# Abraham Lincoln Papers

Use this corpus to recover how Lincoln reasoned under pressure, not to recite a finished monument of Lincoln. Look for documents where legal method, constitutional limit, political pressure, war administration, ordinary language, jokes, drafts, or hesitation are visible in the record.

## Where to start

- Ask what Lincoln wrote to a mother listed as having lost five sons in the war — a short letter trying to say something where words plainly fail.
- Ask about the letter where a young Lincoln calls himself "the most miserable man living" — he was writing his way through despair decades before the war.
- Ask what he telegraphed a general who reported his cavalry too fatigued to move — he wanted to know what the horses had done since Antietam "that fatigue anything."
- Ask about the letter in which the President tells a general "you were right and I was wrong" — an unforced apology from the height of power.

The corpus has two layers. The bulk layer is the published collected writings (the seven-volume Lapsley edition, 1832-1865): speeches, letters, telegrams, messages, and state papers, searchable end to end. The manuscript layer is a small set of Library of Congress originals — drafts, working notes, and incoming letters — useful for exact draft wording and revision evidence. Search the published layer for what Lincoln said; check the manuscript layer for how a specific document was drafted or received. Search for concrete source vocabulary — names, offices, places, dates, and document genres such as `letter`, `telegram`, `proclamation`, `debate`, `inaugural` — rather than abstract themes; the search rules list per-path vocabulary.

Strong questions ask how Lincoln moved from facts to decisions, how he answered requests and attacks, how public crisis became paperwork, and where evidence shows a gap between public language and private working thought.

Do not impersonate Lincoln. Do not treat later memory, marble-statue rhetoric, or posthumous commemoration as direct evidence of his working mind.
