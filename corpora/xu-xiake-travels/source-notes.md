# Source Notes

This capsule ships no text, but it is downloadable: the source manifest curates sixteen chapters of the Xu Xiake Youji from Chinese Wikisource (zh.wikisource.org), fetched locally only after the user confirms the rights notice. The download covers the famous early mountain diaries (Tiantai, Yandang, Huangshan twice, Lushan), the core southwest-expedition diaries (Hunan, Guangxi, Guizhou, Yunnan), and the hydrographic treatises (盤江考, 溯江紀源) — a curated selection, not the complete Youji.

Rights, in two layers:

- The seventeenth-century text is public domain everywhere: Xu Xiake died in 1641 and the compilation dates to 1642.
- The Wikisource transcription layer (proofreading, formatting, footnotes) is reusable under CC BY-SA 4.0. Quoting with attribution to Chinese Wikisource satisfies this; the underlying Ming text carries no rights of its own.

Provenance of the text itself:

- Xu Xiake died in 1641; his diaries were compiled by Ji Mengliang (季夢良) in 1642. Parts of the original were lost or damaged during the Ming-Qing transition and were reconstructed from copies, so all editions descend from an imperfect transmission with known gaps.
- The Chinese Wikisource transcription is in traditional characters and is community-proofread rather than a critical edition; check surprising readings against a printed edition before leaning on them.
- Downloaded pages carry small amounts of Wikisource apparatus that survives text extraction: chapter navigation lines at the top, a public-domain license banner near the end, and numbered editorial footnotes (marked [1], [2] inline, listed at the bottom). These are Wikisource editorial material, not Xu Xiake's text — never quote them as diary text.
- The Chinese Text Project (ctext.org) hosts a second full transcription with an explicit OCR-error warning. Its terms discourage bulk automated downloading, so that record stays catalog-only: use it in their interface to compare readings or to reach chapters this capsule does not download.
- Modern punctuated, collated, and annotated editions add editorial work that may carry rights; the user must confirm the rights status of any edition they import beyond the downloaded chapters.

Practical limits to keep in mind: the download is a sixteen-chapter selection, so absence from search is weak evidence — the full Youji has many more chapters (three of four Guangxi parts and ten of thirteen Yunnan parts are not downloaded); transcription errors are possible; the text is traditional-character; and dates run on the Chinese calendar in Chongzhen reign years. Any English rendering quoted in answers is a translation made at answer time, not part of the source, and should be labeled as such.

Downloaded and imported documents stay local, are never bundled, and are indexed only into a locally generated SQLite file.
