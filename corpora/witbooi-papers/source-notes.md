# Source Notes

The curated core is the DBNL (Digitale Bibliotheek voor de Nederlandse Letteren) digitization of "Die dagboek van Hendrik Witbooi, Kaptein van die Witbooi-Hottentotte 1884-1905," The Van Riebeeck Society, Cape Town, 1929, edited from the original documents in the government archive at Windhoek, with a foreword by Gustav Voigts. The original manuscript letter-book dates from 1884-1893 and was seized by German troops at Hoornkrans on 12 April 1893; the printed edition adds correspondence through the 1894 Naukluft campaign. The original letter-journals, held by the National Archives of Namibia, were inscribed on the UNESCO Memory of the World register in 2005.

Known limits of this edition:

- Colonial bias in the apparatus: the English introduction, chronology, and Voigts foreword speak in the settlers' voice, call the 1904 rising treacherous, and use the period pejorative "Hottentot." Witbooi's own letters must be separated from this frame.
- Coverage: Witbooi's own texts run 1884-1894. The 1904-1905 rising and his death near Vaalgras (29 October 1905) are narrated only by the editors, not by Witbooi.
- Language layers: letters in nonstandard Cape Dutch, footnotes in Afrikaans, introduction in English. Quotations usually need an English translation supplied by the answer, and translation itself introduces uncertainty.
- Transcription artifacts: the DBNL text is a diplomatic transcription; page markers such as "{==22==}" and "{>>pagina-aanduiding<<}" and numbered footnote references share the text stream with the letters, and OCR-era oddities may survive.
