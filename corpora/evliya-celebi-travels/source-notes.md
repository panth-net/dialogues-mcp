# Source Notes

The curated core is the two-volume "Narrative of Travels in Europe, Asia, and Africa, in the Seventeenth Century" (Oriental Translation Fund, 1834 and 1850), Joseph von Hammer's English translation of the opening of Evliya Celebi's Seyahatname.

Known limits of this edition:

- These two volumes are a small fraction of the ten-volume Seyahatname. They cover roughly volume I (Istanbul) and the first journeys of volume II (Bursa, Trabzon, the Caucasus, Azov, the Crimea, Crete, Erzurum, Tabriz). Hammer's separate part containing the famous survey of the Istanbul guilds (vol. I part II, 1846) is not on Project Gutenberg, and volumes III-X — Syria, the Balkans, Hungary, Vienna, the hajj to Mecca, Egypt — were never translated in this series. Absence from a search here is not absence from Evliya's work.
- The nineteenth-century translation is dated and sometimes loose: Hammer abridges, paraphrases, mis-reads names, and smooths the prose to the taste of the 1830s. Treat the English wording as Hammer's, and do not rest fine points on a single translated word.
- Marvels, talismans, huge round numbers, and tall tales are genre features of the seyahatname, not errors: report them as what Evliya wrote and how the genre worked, rather than silently correcting or discarding them.
- Transliterations are pre-modern throughout (Trebisonde, Assov, Brussa, Kostantineh), and the translator's preface and notes share the text stream with Evliya's narrative.
