# Leo Tolstoy Diaries

## What this corpus is

This corpus is Tolstoy's private diary in the original Russian. The genre is moral self-accounting: he opened the diary in March 1847, a nineteen-year-old university dropout in a Kazan clinic, and closed it in November 1910, days before dying at Astapovo railway station. In between he used it to set rules of life, record failures against them, count losses at cards, argue with God, draft ideas for fiction, and keep score against his own appetites. The text comes from diary volumes of the 90-volume Jubilee edition (Polnoe sobranie sochinenii, 1928-1958), digitized as searchable PDFs by the Tolstoy Museum's "Vsyo Tolstoy v odin klik" (All of Tolstoy in one click) project.

The curated core is three volumes chosen to span the whole life:

- Volume 46 (1847-1854): Kazan, Yasnaya Polyana estate management, the Caucasus with brother Nikolai, army life at Starogladkovskaya, gambling debts, Franklin-style rules and self-reproach, and the first success — "Detstvo" accepted by Nekrasov's Sovremennik in 1852.
- Volume 52 (1891-1894): the famine kitchens at Begichevka in Ryazan province, the renunciation of copyright in September 1891, the property division with the family, vegetarianism, non-resistance, and the long struggle to write about art and about the Kingdom of God.
- Volume 58 (1910): the final year — the war with Sofia Andreevna over the diaries themselves, Chertkov, the secret will signed in the Grumont forest, the "diary for myself alone" he hid in his boot, the night flight from Yasnaya Polyana on 28 October, Optina Pustyn, Shamordino, and Astapovo.

## Where to start

- Ask what rules of life the nineteen-year-old set for himself — then read the next entry, where he breaks them; the whole diary is a score kept against his own appetites. He wrote in Russian; ask for the original with a translation.
- Ask what the most famous novelist alive thought of his own vanity — he hunts it through his heart like an illness, entry after entry.
- Ask about the "diary for myself alone" he kept hidden in his boot at 82 — by the end, even his diary needed a secret diary.
- Ask what he wrote on 28 October 1910, the day he finally fled his own house — a departure he had been talking himself into for decades.

## How to search

The corpus is in Russian. Search with Russian source vocabulary, not English: search for names (Соня, Чертков, Николенька), places (Ясная Поляна, Бегичевка, Кавказ, Астапово), and Tolstoy's own moral vocabulary (правила, слабость, тщеславие, вера, смерть, уход). English queries will return almost nothing. The search rules file gives starting queries with English glosses.

One mechanical warning: the Jubilee edition expands Tolstoy's abbreviations in square brackets — he wrote "ч[то]", "к[оторый]", "т[ак] к[ак]" — so an exact search for "который" can miss entries where the word is printed as "к[оторый]". Prefer shorter stems and OR-sets.

## How to answer

Fetch entries before making claims, quote the Russian with a translation, and cite the volume and the entry date visible in the passage. Separate evidence from interpretation: a diary vow is evidence that Tolstoy made the vow, not that he kept it — usually the next entries record the breaking. Distinguish Tolstoy's diary text from the Soviet editors' prefaces and commentary, which share the same PDF text stream. Dates are Old Style (Julian), twelve to thirteen days behind the Western calendar.

## Limitations

The searchable text is an ABBYY OCR layer over page scans: hyphenation artifacts, occasional misrecognized characters, and running heads (dates and "ДНЕВНИК") mixed into the text. Each volume also carries editorial apparatus — prefaces, variant readings, notes — so not every hit is Tolstoy's own sentence. Any English rendering in an answer is the assistant's translation, and uncertainty about a hard reading should be said out loud.
